Title:
Strange and charming Higgs decays at the Future Circular Collider

Description:
The Standard Model (SM) of Particle Physics makes precise predictions for the Higgs boson’s interactions with other particles. Deviations between the observed and predicted interaction strengths would signify physics beyond the SM. Since discovery, the ATLAS and CMS experiments at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) have measured the Higgs boson’s interactions with the W and Z boson, the top and bottom quarks, and the tau and muon leptons, with varying degrees of precision. To date, all Higgs boson interactions are found to agree with the SM predictions within experimental uncertainties. The Higgs boson interactions with charm- and strange- quarks remain largely unexplored, however. Searches for the Higgs boson decay to charm have been carried out at the LHC experiments, but would only be sensitive if the Higgs-charm interaction is many times larger than the SM expectation. No such interactions have been observed. The proposed Future Circular Collider (FCC) will directly address this, with measurements of SM-like Higgs-charm interactions at (hopefully) 1% precision. The FCC also intends to observe the interaction between the Higgs boson and strange quarks, but the sensitivity will be much worse due to the much smaller Higgs branching ratio and the difficulties in identifying the flavour of lighter quark decays. In this project, you will investigate strange- and charm- tagging algorithms at the Future Circular Collider. The possible directions of the project could include: (i) evaluating the performance of the existing algorithms and their dependency on the detector performance, (ii) developing new simulations of the FCC detectors and investigating the impact on charm/strange tagging, (iii) evaluating the associated physics modelling uncertainties that arise from different QCD hadronisation models, and/or (iv) developing new machine-learning (transformer) models that are optimised to study Higgs decay to strange and charm quarks. The exact scope and direction of research will be decided by the students after discussions with Prof Pilkington and Dr Costa at the start of the project. In completing the project, you will learn: (i) standard experimental data analysis techniques used by experimental particle physics, (ii) machine-learning techniques for flavour tagging and particle identification, and (iii) how to manipulate the FCC simulation, reconstruction, and analysis chain. You will be working as part of a new consortium between Manchester, Cambridge and Glasgow and will have the opportunity to present your results in wider FCC analysis meetings. Proficiency in programming in Python and/or C++ is essential for the successful completion of the project. Previous experience with particle-physics analysis software or machine learning is an advantage but not essential. Supervision will be via weekly meetings, where students will be expected to present their progress with slides. Plans and problems can be discussed in these meetings or via email. Please contact Andrew.Pilkington@manchester.ac.uk to get further details on the project.
